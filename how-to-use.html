How to use it

From a link to a lasting note

Reading Room is an app. Your own AI coding assistant (Claude Code, Codex CLI, or Gemini CLI) sits behind its buttons — you read a paper once, it writes a focused report into a catalogue you keep. Here's the whole loop.

The rhythm: click a flow — Analyze, Compare, Discuss, or Deep dive — fill in the paper(s), and the assistant writes it while the catalogue rebuilds itself.

Step-by-step walkthrough

1

Open the app, analyze a paper

Double-click ReadingRoom.app (macOS), ReadingRoom.bat (Windows) or ReadingRoom.sh (Linux), then click Analyze paper and paste an arXiv id or a link — or take a PDF from your computer: Choose PDF…, or just drop it on the panel. Most papers outside machine learning aren't on arXiv, and a paywalled one is only ever a file in your Downloads; that file goes into the catalogue the same way. Drop a stack of them, or use Add another, and Reading Room works through the queue one paper at a time. Options sets the depth, the audience and a focus for this run only. Pick which assistant writes it with the claude / codex / gemini pills; the gear next to them (also Terminal ▾ → Assistant settings) sets each assistant's model and reasoning effort once, and the app remembers it. Your assistant reads the whole paper and writes the report; Open report appears when it's ready. While it works, the card shows how long it has been going and the assistant's last line — and you can close the window: the run carries on and finishes writing. If the assistant needs to ask you something, Show terminal reveals the live session.

2

Read the report

Each paper gets its own page: a one-paragraph lead, then focus views as tabs — the ones you chose in Setup, adapted to each paper (Summary and Significance are always written). Equations are real, selectable LaTeX; results land in tidy tables. Set a reading status and a star priority right on the page, open the paper itself with View PDF, and keep your own notes with Edit notes.

3

Browse, search & filter

The home catalogue is instant: full-text search, tag chips, and a To-read / Reading / Read filter. It shows your most recent papers first and keeps the page light.

4

Follow the connections

Open Connections for a force-directed graph of how your papers cite each other. Hollow ghost nodes are important references cited by two or more of your papers but not yet analyzed — click one and Analyze opens the flow with the id already filled in, or Dismiss it so it never resurfaces.

5

Keep your citations straight

The Library is a grouped reference list with one-click BibTeX export, and your own custom tags act as collections. Ask your assistant to refresh venues (/refresh-venues in the Terminal drawer) and Reading Room checks DBLP for any preprint that has since been published, updating the venue + BibTeX while keeping the arXiv link for reading.

6

Compare, discuss & go deeper

The ▾ next to Analyze switches flow — each opens the same kind of overlay, with a paper picker already loaded from your catalogue.

  • Compare — two or three papers side by side. Search the list, tick the papers, and optionally type a question or topic ("which handles long sequences better, and at what cost?") so the whole comparison is organised around answering it.
  • Discuss — a live conversation about a paper in the terminal, so it's the one flow that opens it. End chat saves the compacted chat as a Discussion below the reading list; Stop discussion discards it.
  • Deep dive — a worked derivation, proof or mechanism trace on one idea from a report, written as its own Deep dive tab or merged into a section you pick.
  • What every flow shares — Show terminal reveals the live session if the assistant needs you; Stop discards the run, and nothing half-written is kept; Open appears the moment the result is built.
7

Tune it to you

The avatar in the header opens Profile and Setup. Setup is a short questionnaire — your name, your field(s), the default focus views (add your own), your tag vocabulary, and depth/audience. It works in any discipline, and every report still adapts its sections to the paper. On the last step you launch your assistant — the terminal opens on top of the window; if it asks you to trust the folder or to log in, answer there — and Apply setup writes your profile + field config through it, no command edits needed. Want it to know more? Profile → Edit full profile is a free-form page about your background, what you're working on, and what you want from reports; every report, comparison and discussion reads it.

8

Keep it safe, keep it current

Back up (avatar menu) writes one zip with your reports, notes, comparisons, discussions, settings, optionally the PDFs — and your browser reading state (status, stars, collections). Save it to Downloads, the app's own backups folder, or a path you choose; Restore from zip brings it all back on a new machine (merge, or replace everything). Updates (avatar menu) works however you installed: a Git clone is checked against its remote and fast-forwarded (if it can't be, your assistant finishes the job with /update); a ZIP download is checked against the published version on GitHub, and the latest ZIP is downloaded and unpacked over the app's own files. When a new version is there you choose Later (ask again at the next launch), Skip this version (never again for that version — a newer one will still be offered) or Update now (one click, plus a one-click restart when the app itself changed). Your library, notes and settings are never touched — but taking a Back up first never hurts.

9

Prefer the terminal?

Every button runs a command you can also type yourself: open the Terminal drawer, start your assistant with the ▾, and use /explain-paper, /compare, /learn, /deep-dive, /remove, /setup, /backup or /update — add --approve to skip a command's confirmation gate. Or skip the window entirely with cd workmode && npm start. Same assistant, same subscription, same library.
